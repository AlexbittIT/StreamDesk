# User Feedback Summary

## Feedback method

During this sprint, we collected feedback through four short usability-review sessions. The goal was to check whether the current StreamDesk implementation is understandable and useful for real event-preparation workflows.

The feedback was collected in three formats:

- stakeholder review sessions;
- guided usability testing;
- observation notes during scenario walkthroughs.

Participants were asked to go through the main flows and comment on what felt clear, confusing, useful, or risky. The focus was not only on whether the features exist, but also on whether they make sense as part of the actual product workflow.

## Tested scenarios

The testing focused on the most important product scenarios rather than individual isolated tasks.

### Scenario 1: Working with an estimate

Participants checked whether the estimate flow is understandable as a business document: opening an estimate, reviewing positions, checking grouped items, understanding statuses, and preparing the result for export.

### Scenario 2: Exporting results

Participants reviewed the export flow and discussed whether Excel/PDF output would be useful for sharing estimates outside the platform.

### Scenario 3: AI-assisted actions

Participants reviewed the AI-related flow and commented on whether it is visible, understandable, and trustworthy enough for real use.

### Scenario 4: Equipment and warehouse logic

Participants reviewed the direction where estimate items are matched with warehouse data, including missing equipment, deficit states, and items without a known price.

### Scenario 5: Connection diagram and visual clarity

Participants checked the connection diagram/editor direction, including icons, canvas readability, device blocks, and whether the visual language is understandable.

## Key findings

### 1. The product direction is clear and useful

The stakeholders confirmed that StreamDesk is moving in the right direction. The strongest value is that separate operational pieces are becoming connected: estimates, equipment availability, project context, exports, AI support, and technical diagrams are no longer treated as isolated features.

This was especially important for Sasha and Amal, because they evaluated the product from the point of view of real event preparation. Their main conclusion was that the current direction can reduce manual work and make planning more structured.

### 2. Estimates became more realistic as a product feature

Participants noted that the estimate module now feels closer to real usage. Export, grouped positions, project binding, versions, shift calculations, and warehouse matching make the estimate less like a simple table and more like an operational document.

The most important improvement is that estimate logic is moving away from local or frontend-only behaviour toward a more controlled product flow.

### 3. Export is not optional for real workflows

Stakeholders confirmed that Excel/PDF export is important because estimates are often shared with managers, clients, contractors, or team members outside the system. Even if the platform keeps the main workflow inside StreamDesk, export is still needed for communication and approval.

The next step is to test export on realistic estimate data, not only on small examples.

### 4. AI generation is useful but currently not trusted enough

Participants were interested in AI-assisted generation, but they did not fully trust it yet. The main problem is that AI actions are not visible enough in the interface, and it is not always clear what was generated by AI, what was edited manually, and what should be checked by the user.

The feedback was clear: AI should behave like an assistant, not like a black box. The interface should show the source of the result, possible errors, and what still needs manual review.

### 5. Design needs visual cleanup

The most repeated usability issue was visual readability. Several participants mentioned that the current design feels too contrast-heavy in some places. The interface attracts attention, but not always in the right way.

Icons were also mentioned as a weak point. Some of them are difficult to read quickly, especially in the connection diagram and technical views. For a tool that depends on visual interpretation, icon clarity is important.

### 6. Error states should be explicit

Participants preferred visible error states over silent fallbacks. This applies to AI generation, warehouse matching, export, and estimate calculations.

For example, if AI fails, the system should not pretend that everything is fine. If an item has no price or is missing from the warehouse, the user should see that clearly. If calculation rules are incomplete, the UI should explain what happened.

### 7. Warehouse matching adds strong industrial value

The warehouse matching direction was evaluated positively. Participants said that estimates become much more useful when the system can show whether equipment is available, missing, or present without a price.

This is one of the most practical industrial improvements because it connects planning with real operational resources.

## Problems found

The main problems found during feedback sessions were:

- the design is sometimes too contrast-heavy;
- some icons are hard to read and need simplification;
- AI generation is not visible enough in the interface;
- users do not fully trust AI output without clear source and validation markers;
- export should be tested on larger and more realistic estimates;
- warehouse deficit and “no price” cases should stay clearly visible;
- shift calculation rules need predictable server-side handling;
- bug fixes from the current sprint should be retested as full user flows, not only as separate tasks.

## Conclusions

The feedback was generally positive. Stakeholders confirmed that the product direction is valuable and that the current sprint made StreamDesk closer to a real industrial tool. The strongest improvements are connected estimates, export, warehouse-aware planning, AI-assisted actions, and clearer technical workflows.

At the same time, the review showed that the next step should focus on trust and clarity. The product should make AI output more transparent, reduce visual noise, improve icon readability, and test estimate/export scenarios on realistic data.

The main conclusion is that the product is no longer just a set of separate features. It is starting to behave like a connected workflow for event preparation, but it still needs polish, clearer feedback states, and stronger validation before it can be considered stable.

## Follow-up actions

- Reduce excessive visual contrast in the main working screens.
- Redraw or simplify unclear icons.
- Make AI generation more visible in the interface.
- Add source/status indicators for AI-generated results.
- Keep AI failure states explicit instead of hiding them behind fallback behaviour.
- Retest export on realistic estimate data.
- Keep warehouse deficit and “no price” states visible.
- Validate shift calculation rules on day/night/weekend cases.
- Retest current bug fixes through full user scenarios.
- Continue collecting stakeholder feedback after the next deployment/review.
